Analytical Question: Report your overall churn rate and your churn rate by contract type, using your 
own numbers. Month-to-month customers almost always churn more than annual-contract customers in 
this data — explain in one paragraph why this is a correlation, not proof that switching everyone to 
annual contracts would cause the same drop in churn (think about who self-selects into month-to-month 
in the first place).

Explanation:

This pattern is a correlation, not proof that switching everyone
to annual contracts would cause the same drop in churn. 
Customers who choose month-to-month plans are not random—they 
tend to be newer, more price-sensitive, less committed, or 
less satisfied with the service than customers who opt for 
annual contracts, while annual customers are often more 
established and more loyal. In other words, contract type is 
partly a self-selection variable: the people who choose 
month-to-month likely differ in important ways from those 
who choose annual plans, so the higher churn in month-to-month
contracts may be driven by those underlying differences rather
than the contract itself causing churn. 
   To establish causality, we would need to compare otherwise
similar customers while controlling for factors like tenure, 
service type, satisfaction, and customer behavior.
