# Task 7: Executive Memo

## CFO headline

"Our 26.54% churn rate is concentrated financially in the New, High-Spend, High-Risk
segment, where 1,937 customers at 59.37% predicted risk and $83.43 average monthly spend
represent approximately $95,944 in expected monthly revenue at risk."

## First campaign target and trade-off

Fund the retention campaign for **New, High-Spend, High-Risk** customers first. This segment
has the highest risk (59.37%) and high average spend ($83.43), so its estimated exposure is
the largest: $95,944 per month, calculated as 1,937 customers x $83.43 x 0.5937. The trade-off
is campaign efficiency: **New, Low-Spend, High-Risk** customers also need attention, but their
lower $37.22 average spend produces only about $15,559 of expected monthly revenue at risk;
targeting Segment 2 first prioritizes dollars protected rather than risk percentage alone.

## Monitoring signal

Track the **four-week realized churn rate among customers flagged as New, High-Spend,
High-Risk**, split before and after any price change or new competitor offer; if realized
churn rises materially above the model's predicted risk or the historical segment baseline,
the customer behavior has shifted and the model should be retrained with the new pricing and
competitive context.

## Governance

To prove Clause 4's non-discrimination rule from logs alone, retain an immutable record for
every explanation containing the customer-field allowlist and forbidden-field scan result,
prompt hash, output scan result, clause ID/text, model and prompt versions, timestamp, and
request ID, then audit that every record shows a clean scan and only the approved fields in
the prompt and output.

## Cost control

Cache the explanation by a versioned, deterministic key made from clause ID, risk band,
tenure band, and the three approved feature names, and reuse the cached response for matching
weekly profiles while retaining the clause ID/text and prompt hash as the citation record;
this reduces duplicate Gemini calls without weakening the retrieval-grounding guarantee.

## Implementation explanation

### TotalCharges and train/test trust

The raw `TotalCharges` column contained blanks and non-numeric values, so I converted it
with `pd.to_numeric(errors='coerce')`; invalid entries became missing values instead of
silently remaining strings. I then filled numeric missing values with the column median,
which kept those customers usable without letting a few large bills determine the
replacement value. The logistic-regression model achieved 0.8026 training accuracy and
0.7977 test accuracy, a gap of only 0.0049, so the similar results do not suggest a large
overfitting problem; I would still judge the model using recall and ROC AUC as well as
accuracy because churn is the minority class.

### Why Segment 2 came first

I recommended **New, High-Spend, High-Risk** customers because the decision was based on
both likelihood and value, not risk alone: 1,937 customers had 59.37% average predicted
risk and $83.43 average monthly charges, producing about $95,944 of expected monthly
revenue at risk. The competing New, Low-Spend, High-Risk segment had a lower $37.22
average monthly charge and about $15,559 at risk, so Segment 2 offers the larger financial
upside even though both segments deserve retention attention; the trade-off is that a
campaign may cost more per customer to protect higher-value accounts.

### Retrieval test and compliance impact

The retrieval-free Gemini call was coded to ask for a clause number without supplying the
playbook text, but it was not executed because `GEMINI_API_KEY` was unavailable; therefore
there is no exact model response or verified correctness result to report. The intended
failure mode is an **ungrounded clause-number hallucination**: a confident policy choice
without the policy source. That matters more here than in casual chat because a wrong
clause can authorize an inappropriate contract or discount, create inconsistent customer
treatment, cause financial or regulatory exposure, and leave no defensible evidence that
the approved retention policy was followed.

### Exact LLM inputs and demographic protection

The grounded call received this system instruction: produce exactly 3 or 4 sentences for a
retention agent, use only the retrieved playbook clause and supplied feature names, do not
invent facts or actions, and never mention `gender`, `SeniorCitizen`, `Partner`,
`Dependents`, or any proxy or implication of them. Its user prompt contained only the
retrieved clause text, the customer's risk probability, tenure in months, and the three
filtered top feature names; it did not contain the customer's full row or demographic
values. The demographic columns were kept out of the LLM prompt in the `forbidden_terms`
filter, which removes feature names containing those four terms before `top_features` is
constructed; the system prompt repeats the prohibition as a second control. The model may
still use those columns internally because `model_features` drops only `Churn` and
`customerID`, so removing them from the LLM explanation is separate from removing them from
the predictive model.
